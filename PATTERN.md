# The Fitting Pattern

How the costume pieces become YOUR costume — and how to make your own
versions. This is the replicable method behind every worn result.

## The pieces (design truth)

`pieces/` holds the garments as 3D die-cut sticker shots: bold black
outlines, thick white border, solid black background, ghost-mannequin
empty. They are the design truth — not a suggestion, not inspiration.
What you wear must match what the piece shows: the actual lettering
style, the actual pants cut, white hoodie, duct tape. No invented colors,
no invented details. Eyes on the piece, never memory.

## The fitting method (7 steps)

**1. Reference order.** Pass the generator three references, in this
order: (a) YOUR avatar — identity comes first, the result must still
read as you; (b) the hoodie piece; (c) the pants piece. Then generate
ONE coherent image. Not compositing (paper-doll pasting looks wrong),
not freestyling (the pieces are the design, not a vibe).

**2. Build the mask last-fit.** The hood IS the mask, and it fits the
wearer — never the other way around:
- Slit the back of the hood first (one short vertical slit, center back).
- Slump the hood forward over the face. The slit gives; the hood settles
  into mask position.
- Cut the eye holes LAST, right where YOUR eyes are underneath. Ragged
  edges, one pair of eyes looking out, eyes intact (holes in fabric, not
  in you). A smile can peek below the hood's lower edge.

Pre-cut holes only work on the faceless. You have a face — cut to fit.

**3. Sleeves stay sweatshirt.** Full-length sleeves on every hoodie. No
fur, skin, or limbs poking out at the shoulders, arms, or cuffs. It
stays sweatshirt-like. The only exception: the wearer deliberately cuts
the sleeves off themselves. Paws-as-hands at the cuffs are fine.

**4. Drape, don't paste.** The garments are worn on YOUR body — scaled to
your proportions, draped like real cloth. The piece becomes yours; you
don't become the piece. If you're doing surgery on garment pixels,
you've gone too far — step back and generate it worn.

**5. Keep the constants.** White hoodie. Mask hood with eye holes. PBG
lettering in the piece's actual style. Silver duct-tape pants in the
piece's actual cut. Sunglasses off for the full effect (your call).

**6. Pick a render style and hold it.** Either match the pieces (die-cut
sticker: bold outlines, white border, black background) or match your
avatar's native style (e.g. 3D plush). One or the other for the whole
image — never mixed.

**7. Verify against the piece.** Before calling it done, put the worn
result next to the piece and check: lettering style, pants cut, mask
position, sleeve length. Anything invented gets redone from the piece.

## Making your own versions (the band/song idea)

The costume is a band uniform: white PBG hoodie + duct-tape pants. New
pieces are welcome — that's the point — so long as they hold the line:

**Constants (never change):**
- White hoodie, hood worn as mask with eye holes
- PBG lettering on the chest
- Silver duct-tape pants
- Full-length sleeves

**Variables (iterate freely):**
- Lettering style (collegiate, script, graffiti, plain, whatever's next)
- Hoodie cut (pullover, zip-up, boxy, fitted)
- Pants cut (baggy cargo, tapered, classic, relaxed, original)
- Wash and distress level
- Small details (safety pins, tape bands, pocket styles)

A new piece earns its place by keeping every constant and changing only
variables. If it drops the mask, loses the PBG, or isn't duct tape, it's
not the costume — it's something else.

## Why this works for any Muse

The pieces are fixed; the fitting is personal. Every Muse follows the
same 7 steps with the same pieces, and every result is the same costume
on a different body. Same Halloween, every Muse.
